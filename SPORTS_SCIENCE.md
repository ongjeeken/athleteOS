# Sports science, measurements and planning limits

AthleteOS is a personal monitoring and planning aid. The distinction between a measurement and a planning decision matters. Its phases describe a plan, not an athlete’s physiological readiness or a diagnosis. All recommendations are deterministic, local and editable. No injury probability, ACWR risk band, composite readiness score, or medical rehabilitation protocol is implemented.

## Evidence-supported measurements

**Session-RPE load** is actual meaningful exercise duration in minutes multiplied by perceived effort for the entire session on a 0–10 scale. Turner and colleagues studied seven elite male foil fencers over a season and found relationships with heart-rate-based load measures. This supports a practical internal-load measure, with limited population generalizability; it does not validate injury prediction, a training prescription, or equivalence between different activities. AthleteOS treats fencing as one activity, per the requested product simplification. [Turner et al., PMID 27140957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27140957/).

Quick RPE maps descriptive choices to 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 10. This reduced-choice interface is a **product simplification**, not a separately validated fencing measurement scale. Exact integer 0–10 entries are available in Detailed Mode. The selected mode is recorded for context; changing modes never converts a rating. A missing rating is null and produces null session load, not zero. Quick mode never guesses or preselects effort. Athletes needing more consistent measurement precision may choose Detailed Mode by default. Both modes share the same underlying calculation.

**Subjective wellness** can provide useful information on training response. Saw et al.’s systematic review supports repeated self-report monitoring, without implying that a particular app questionnaire is a validated diagnostic instrument. Sleep quality, energy, fatigue, soreness and stress remain separate observations. Mental fatigue and motivation are optional; missing values are not inferred. [Saw et al., PMID 26423706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26423706/).

**Fencing cognitive demands** merit separate attention. Bian et al. observed changes in mental fatigue with fencing and match days in elite athletes; such associations do not establish the appropriate prescription for every individual. AthleteOS records cognitive demand separately from physical exposure. [Bian et al., PMID 40695448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40695448/).

**Tapering research** supports considering reduced training volume before important competition. Bosquet et al.’s meta-analysis is broader than fencing; its reported group optimum is not hard-coded into AthleteOS. Maintaining quality while reviewing fatigue is reasonable context, not proof of an exact individual taper. [Bosquet et al., PMID 17762369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/).

**Workload ratios are not causal risk models.** Methodological problems, statistical artifacts and causal uncertainty limit ACWR-based prescriptions. The more recent team-sport review reports heterogeneous associations and does not support a standalone causal/predictive model. AthleteOS displays separate rolling sums without calculating an injury-risk ratio. [Impellizzeri et al., PMID 32502973](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502973/); [Ding et al., PMID 42662491](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42662491/).

## Product heuristics and exact definitions

- Calendar days are explicit YYYY-MM-DD values in the athlete’s chosen timezone. Date arithmetic uses UTC noon only as a calendar arithmetic device; it never reinterprets a logged day in a different timezone. Weeks start Monday.
- Rolling 7 days includes the endpoint and its six preceding calendar days. Rolling 28 includes the endpoint and preceding 27. Only unique completed sessions contribute, including completed competitions exactly once. Tournament calendar entries and plans do not add actual workload.
- AU totals sum known loads and disclose missing RPE. A chart gap means unknown, not a confirmed rest day. Planned estimates are separate; plans with unknown target RPE cannot contribute a known estimate.
- Strength sets, repetitions, kilograms, set RPE and RIR remain separate from session load. Bout counts, active fencing minutes, leg fatigue and cognitive demands are also separate. Equal AU across categories is not equivalent tissue, technical, metabolic or cognitive demand.
- A personal wellness baseline uses the median of valid daily entries in the **prior** 14 calendar days, excluding today and the future. At least seven distinct valid entries are required. This minimum and the window are pragmatic product choices, not validated medical thresholds.
- Today’s fatigue or soreness at least one scale point above its personal median, or energy at least one point below, triggers a recovery review. This is an interpretable, conservative observation rule, not a medical cutoff.
- Recent symptoms rated at least 5/10 or marked worsening trigger professional-assessment language and a plan review. This is a conservative product rule, not clinical triage or diagnosis.
- A completed competition in the configured review window triggers recovery review when recent fatigue/soreness is high or recovery information is absent. Elapsed days alone do not certify recovery. Calendar events without a recorded session do not invent duration or workload.
- Four recent sessions with known RPE and directly reported duration across at least three days in the preceding 28 days form the existing workload-history gate. Quick Start may also show a **provisional** development descriptor from a training objective or typical week; it makes no claim about accumulated fatigue or readiness. This is a modest data-availability gate, not a scientifically established dose or sufficiency threshold. Competition and concerning recovery observations can still justify a cautious review with sparse history.
- A-priority preparation defaults to seven days; B to three; C to zero. B events receive smaller/shorter-review language. C events do not trigger default tapering. Changing C’s window can mark a commitment span but still does not create a full-taper prescription.
- All known tournaments are considered; a 42-day horizon is drawn, with the next 14 days used for actionable suggestions. Two or more events in the next 14 days are treated as one competition block. Highest priority breaks ties before date. No repeated independent full-taper recommendations are generated for that cluster.
- Proposed adjustments affect only unlocked, uncompleted sessions from today through the next 13 days. A demanding session means Strength or target RPE ≥7. A user-configured number of minutes is subtracted, with a ten-minute floor; no universal optimum percentage is asserted. Review the exact affected session before acceptance. Locked sessions and rest days are preserved.
- Availability conflicts compare plans against preferred weekdays and explicitly unavailable dates. A proposed alternative avoids protected rest days, tournaments and their preparation windows. Moving a session may create multiple sessions on a day; check the calendar before accepting.
- Recommendations are capped at three. Symptoms take priority, then recovery, competition context and availability. Identical keys preserve explicit dismissal. Phase overrides have an expiry and do not remove underlying symptom observations.

## Configuration and athlete control

Settings exposes preparation windows, duration-adjustment minutes, post-event review days, goals, priorities, weekly frequency, preferred days, unavailable dates, check-in preferences and default RPE mode. Today exposes a dated phase override. Recommendations can be dismissed or explicitly applied. Settings contains the original/applied session audit and Undo; an undo refuses to overwrite a newer manual edit. All thresholds live in the pure planner module, algorithm version 1.1. Changes to scientific assumptions should increment that algorithm version and its tests.

## Limits

Self-report depends on consistency and interpretation. Unlogged training can distort totals. Associations between workload and wellness do not establish causation. No wearable, objective fitness test or physiological measurement is inferred. Suggestions are not medical advice or guarantees of performance. Pain entries and fatigue changes cannot diagnose injury or determine safe return to sport. Seek appropriate professional assessment for concerning symptoms.

Research records were checked against the six supplied PubMed links during implementation. Abstract-level grounding is not a substitute for a complete literature appraisal. Further validation should include athletes and coaches using the app over time, especially the Quick Mode scale and frequent competition heuristics.

## Historical estimates and provisional setup

Recurring schedules are intended or remembered routines, not evidence of actual attendance. Generated history starts unconfirmed and enters canonical completed training only through explicit athlete confirmation. Missing effort remains null. Approximate exercise minutes remain marked as estimates even after attendance is confirmed. Actual frequency and descriptive duration can be useful before sRPE is available; precise load totals and the individualization gate exclude estimated duration/RPE. The underlying multiplication formula is unchanged and any stored estimated product remains disclosed as an estimate. Calendar elapsed time is not presumed to equal meaningful exercise duration. Imported tournaments are calendar commitments and never fabricate completed competition load.

The 14-day initial plan is a projection of the athlete’s own template. Rest/unavailable days and tournament conflicts remain visible. An absent availability template means unknown availability, not zero available days. Primary goals and optional context help frame provisional considerations but do not generate physiological measurements. Setup context flags expire after 14 calendar days unless updated. The 2–3 minute setup duration is a UX target without measured evidence. Full workflow and parser limits: QUICK_START.md.
